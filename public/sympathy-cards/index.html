What to write in a sympathy card

There’s no perfect wording. Short, sincere, and personal is best. Share a memory if you have one, and offer support. A few starting points:

If you knew the person

Share one memory that shows who they were.

“I’ll always remember Walt handing us a bag of apples every fall. He was one of a kind.”

If you didn’t know them

Focus on your friend and what they’re carrying.

“I’m so sorry about your mom. I’m thinking of you and here whenever you need me.”

For the loss of a pet

Honor the bond — it’s real grief.

“Sunny was such a good dog, and so loved. I’m sorry your family is missing him.”

From a team or coworkers

Keep it warm and simple.

“We are holding your family in our thoughts. Luis made every morning brighter here.”

Offering help

Make the offer specific.

“I’d like to bring dinner on Thursday. No need to reply — I’ll leave it at the door.”

Later on

A card weeks later means a lot too.

“Thinking of you as the weeks go by. You don’t have to be okay — I’m still here.”

Sympathy card questions

How much does a card cost?

Creating and previewing a card is free. Sending it by email or text uses 3 credits, and new accounts get enough free credits for their first send. Credit packs start at $5 for 10 credits. A printed card mailed by USPS is $5 (uses 10 credits).

Will the card say “Happy” or anything inappropriate?

No. Set the occasion to sympathy and Genie keeps the artwork and message gentle and respectful. You can review and edit every word before sending.

Can the card honor the person who passed?

Yes. Share something they loved — a place, a hobby, a garden — and Genie can paint it into the cover. Many people choose a peaceful scene without people.

Can I change the message or the picture?

Yes. Edit the inside message yourself for free, switch between short, medium, and long versions, or ask Genie to redo the cover for 1 credit.
