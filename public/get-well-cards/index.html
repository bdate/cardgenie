What to write in a get well card

Keep it warm, short, and hopeful. Skip medical details and focus on them, not the illness. A few starting points:

For a friend

Point to something fun you’ll do together soon.

“The court isn’t the same without you. Rest up — our rematch is waiting.”

For a family member

Let them know they’re missed and loved.

“The house is too quiet without your laugh. Take all the time you need — we’re here.”

For a coworker

Keep it light and take work off their mind.

“Everything here is covered. Your only job is to rest (and send puns).”

For a child

Be playful and promise a treat.

“Brave girl! Extra ice cream is officially doctor’s orders.”

For a long recovery

Offer patience and steady support.

“One day at a time. We’re cheering for every small win along the way.”

For someone who’s far away

Bridge the distance with a memory.

“Sending a hug from across the miles and a promise of a long walk together soon.”

Get well card questions

How much does a card cost?

Creating and previewing a card is free. Sending it by email or text uses 3 credits, and new accounts get enough free credits for their first send. Credit packs start at $5 for 10 credits. A printed card mailed by USPS uses 10 credits.

Can I send it to someone in the hospital?

Yes. Email or text delivery reaches them on their phone instantly. You can also have a printed card mailed to their home or another address.

Can the card look like them?

Yes. Describe how they look, or add a clear close-up photo, and Genie draws them on the cover in the art style you choose. Looks are used for the artwork only — they won’t show up in the inside message.

Can I change the message or the picture?

Yes. Edit the inside message yourself for free, switch between short, medium, and long versions, or ask Genie to redo the cover for 1 credit.
